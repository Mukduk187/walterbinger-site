# Homepage Mechanic Notes

Date: 2026-07-19

This supersedes the quick "custom business card" prototype as a direction. That prototype was rejected because it still behaved like a personal brand homepage: text first, explanation first, illustration second.

Authority note: this file should be read through `docs/WALTER_DIRECTIVES.md`. Walter's direct commands override Scout/ChatGPT commentary or any interpretive language below.

Walter's current direct homepage command is:

> It should almost look like just stars, but then when you hover over one, it turns into a flash tattoo choice board, connected with a constellation. Each star in the set expands to show the image hiding beneath it. Expanding one expands the others connected to it, depending on which lens you have selected, for instance Location + Time, or Maine and Argentina.

## Core Correction

The page should open like a nearly blank field of marks: warm white paper, scattered hand-drawn stars, one restrained magenta trace, and very little copy. The illustration is the navigation, not decoration.

At rest, the visitor should not be told what the site means. They should notice a star and wonder what is hiding under it.

## At Rest

Default state:

- warm white field
- scattered black-ink or graphite stars
- small `Walter Binger` mark, likely in a corner
- one magenta marker trace that feels alive but restrained
- no hero statement
- no cards
- no conventional CTA buttons
- no visible portfolio grid
- no explanatory labels until interaction earns them

It should almost look unfinished.

## Primary Interaction

A star is not a link to a card.

A star is the pinpoint center of a hidden drawing.

When the visitor hovers, focuses, taps, or lingers:

1. The star lights like a Lite-Brite peg.
2. The star expands outward.
3. A black-ink flash illustration draws itself around that point.
4. Related stars illuminate.
5. The magenta marker connects the set with constellation lines.
6. The cluster becomes a temporary flash-tattoo choice board.
7. Labels appear only after the drawings appear.

The drawing should feel like something discovered beneath the star, not an icon that was already sitting there.

## Flash-Sheet Rule

Each concept needs one icon that could genuinely work as a simple flash tattoo:

- clear silhouette
- black ink
- minimal interior detail
- recognizable at thumbnail scale
- slightly strange or personal
- not generic corporate symbolism

Avoid:

- teamwork icon
- process arrows
- healthcare cross
- lightbulb for ideas
- generic map pin
- generic gear

Better candidates:

- pizza slice on a tiny planet
- root wrapped around a sewer pipe
- telescope emerging from a notebook
- public bathroom under a constellation
- lobster buoy attached to a star
- empanada with an orbit line
- a web writing in the sky
- marker line circling a missed detail

## Place, Then Explanation

The first interaction should create curiosity, not clarity.

The visitor should discover where they want to go before the site tells them what anything means.

Possible first-star reveals:

- pizza -> oven glow -> Pizza World
- tree -> roots and hidden infrastructure -> Village Green
- telescope -> rearranged stars -> Observatory
- sewer worker -> underground route -> The Trap

Clicking a revealed drawing moves into its world. Other drawings collapse gently back into stars while the selected drawing expands into the next scene.

## Lenses

The drawings can stay fixed. The selected lens changes which relationships become visible.

This is the useful version of gravitational lensing.

Possible lenses:

- Place + Time
- People
- Work
- Hospitality
- Family
- Systems
- Gratitude
- Conflict
- What Changed?
- What Connects?

The lens should not behave like a database filter. Unselected stars remain present but dormant.

The selected lens changes:

- which nearby stars awaken
- which constellation lines appear
- which cluster names emerge
- which colors or light frequencies become visible
- which route feels inviting

The lens selector should feel like choosing how to look, not filtering a portfolio.

Possible visual forms:

- small hand-drawn optical instrument
- rotating lens wheel
- spectacles in the page margin
- telescope dial
- magnifying glass with a magenta handle

Under `Place + Time`, selecting Maine could connect:

- childhood or family memory

Selecting Argentina could connect:

- hotel cafe

- another period of Walter's life

Under another lens, Maine and Argentina may connect through Hospitality, Food, Home, Reinvention, Community, or Work.

Same stars.

Same drawings.

Different constellation.

## Interaction States

Dormant:

- one tiny star
- no explanation

Curious:

- star brightens as the visitor approaches
- faint hidden outline becomes detectable

Revealed:

- hidden flash drawing sketches itself outward from the star

Related:

- nearby stars awaken and reveal smaller linked drawings

Constellated:

- magenta threads connect the relevant drawings according to the selected lens

Entered:

- selected drawing expands into the next scene
- unrelated drawings collapse gently back into stars

## Copy Rule

Remove 80% of explanatory copy from the first screen.

Possible first screen copy:

> Walter Binger

That may be enough.

Explanatory lines like `I build useful things in messy human systems` can exist deeper in the site, after curiosity has already been created. They should not be the homepage's opening move.

## Structural References

Use these structurally, not as decoration:

- Where the Sidewalk Ends: sparse, funny, black-ink, white-space-driven, stranger than it first appears.
- The Phantom Tollbooth: ordinary doorway -> absurd world -> everything turns out to be connected.
- Le Petit Prince: small worlds with emotional logic.
- Flash tattoo sheets: compact black-ink symbols with personal charge.
- Lite-Brite: tiny points of light reveal a bigger hidden image.

The key question:

> If Walter were eight years old and someone handed him a magenta marker, what world would he draw before anyone told him what a website was?

That is the homepage.
